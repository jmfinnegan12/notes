# How to Change Your Mind by Michael Pollan

# Summary
Filled with interesting facts. Most interesting in my opinion was about the sub-field of psychedelic research within psychology, specifically the lost body of knowledge that was suppressed/discarded after psychedelics were banned in the 60s. Also interesting was the wide variety of possible applications for both people who are unwell (addiction, depression, end of life anxiety) AND the people who are well (enhanced states of consciousness and expanding perspective/humanity). 

I thought Pollan's writing style was extremely verbose and pretentious; the book could have been half as long and accomplished the goal equally as (or more) effectively. I do however give him credit for his thorough research and open minded and objective approach of a culturally charged subject. 

Overall I would not recommend this book. This book covers topics that I am extremely interested in and passionate about: drug policy (specifically failed drug policy), psychology and alternative medicine, mindfulness and the nature of consciousness; but only managed to keep me mildly interested. Pollan's attitude toward the topic is that of an expert giving an open-palm shoulder shrug: there is no common thread or unifying valuable insight, it is just a well-researched collection of facts organized in a somewhat logical order. Aside from the anecdotes about individual trips, most all of the themes of which I have heard before, I came away from the book with no more knowledge than I could have gathered from a 10-20min wikipedia deep dive. The lack of a substantive argument from the author or otherwise compelling common thread and the fact that new science and policy will likely render this book outdated in the near future are the two reasons I would spend my time reading something else.
